From two square waves to Rsoil

How iq, spectrum and moisture actually measure the electrode. Every trace below is simulated from the real circuit (R15 = 1 kΩ, Cp = 8.8 pF, 100 kΩ / 1 nF filters, 16 MHz timer) using media measured on board 1, and processed by the firmware's own fitting code ported line for line.

Between the plates

One timer makes both square waves

TIMER3 counts 16 MHz ticks and wraps every N ticks, so the drive runs at 16 MHz ÷ N. Four compare registers watch the count. Each match fires a PPI channel that toggles a pin through GPIOTE. The CPU is not involved, so every edge lands on an exact tick.

  • DRV (P0.07): CC0 at N/2 and CC5 at N. High for the first half of every period. CC5 also clears the count.
  • READ_CNTL (P0.17), the chopper: CC1 at k and CC2 at k + N/2. High for half a period starting at tick k.

Moving k slides the chopper against the drive in steps of 360°/N. That one number is the phase control. drive N k sets it by hand; iq, sweep and spectrum step it for you.

Firmware: frontEndStart(), lines 542–606.

The electrode bends the wave

DRV feeds the SENSE node through R15. U3 connects one plate to SENSE and grounds the other. Whatever is between the plates loads SENSE, together with Cp, the node's own 8.8 pF.

So SENSE is DRV through a divider:

H(f) = 1 / (1 + R15 · (Ye + j2πf·Cp))

A wetter or more capacitive medium does two things to SENSE. It shrinks it (|H| below 1) and delays it (∠H below 0). Those two numbers, at eight frequencies, are everything the firmware measures.

Switch the medium above to watch the waveform change.

The chopper turns the wave into one DC voltage

U4 sits between SENSE and a 100 kΩ / 1 nF filter. While READ_CNTL is low the filter sees SENSE; while it is high, the filter input is parked on ground. The filter's 100 µs time constant spans about a hundred drive periods, so it settles to the average of that chopped wave.

That average is the overlap between SENSE and the chopper's low half. Slide k and the overlap changes.

After each phase change the firmware waits 5 ms (50 time constants). Then the SAADC averages 64 samples of SENSE_OUT and 64 of DRV_OUT.

U4's second channel does the same to DRV itself. DRV_OUT is the same measurement with nothing in the way: H = 1.

Firmware: runPhaseSet() and readFrontEnd(), lines 647–729.

Step the phase, then fit I and Q

One DC reading is one point. Step k around the period and the readings trace a wave. DRV_OUT traces a triangle, two square waves sliding past each other. SENSE traces a smaller, rounder, shifted copy.

The firmware fits V(θ) = P + I·cos θ + Q·sin θ, with θ = 360°·k/N. With evenly spaced phases the fit is just sums: P is the mean, I = (2/M)·ΣV·cos θ, Q = (2/M)·ΣV·sin θ. Amplitude A = √(I² + Q²), phase φ = atan2(Q, I).

  • iq: 4 phases.
  • spectrum: M phases per frequency, the largest of 16, 12, 8 or 6 that divides N.
  • sweep: all N phases, printing every reading and the fit residual.

φ sits near 180° because the filter only sees SENSE during the chopper's low half. That offset is a convention; step 5 removes it.

With 4 phases DRV_OUT reads A = 825 mV; with 16 it reads 677 mV. Four points cannot tell the triangle's 3rd, 5th and 7th harmonics from its fundamental, so they fold in. More phases, less folding.

Divide SENSE by DRV_OUT

Inside spectrum, each channel's M readings become one complex number, X1 = Σ V·e−jθ = (M/2)·(I − jQ). The measured transfer is H = X1(SENSE) ÷ X1(DRV_OUT).

Both channels share the U4 switch, the filter design, the SAADC, the drive amplitude and the phase steps. Dividing cancels all of it: ADC scale, VDD, the 180° window offset, and any delay both paths see. What remains is the electrode divider.

On the plot the division is a rotation and rescale that puts DRV_OUT at 1∠0°. SENSE lands on H.

Inferred, not measured: board 1's iq phases sit 2–4° below this simulation in every medium (towel 190.5° vs 193.3°, hand 221.5° vs 223.4°). If that offset is switch or edge timing that DRV_OUT shares, this division cancels it.

Firmware: specMeasure(), lines 830–850.

Repeat at eight frequencies, both ways round

spectrum ab runs that measurement at N = 64, 48, 32, 24, 16, 12, 8 and 6: 250 kHz to 2.67 MHz. Each phase costs the 5 ms settle plus about 1.5 ms of ADC reads, so one orientation (102 phases) takes about 0.7 s.

Then U3 swaps the plates (B hot, A grounded) and the sweep runs again. The two complex results are averaged at each frequency. On board 1 the whole command, with fitting and BLE output, takes about 5 s.

At low frequency the solder masks block the medium. Higher frequencies pass through the masks and see the medium's resistance. Where that change happens, and how deep it goes, is what the fit reads.

With Open selected this is spectrum open: one sweep with both plates floating, which measures Cp.

Firmware: specSweep() and runSpectrumFit(), lines 852–1067.

Fit Rsoil, Cm and Cd

The firmware fits three numbers to the eight averaged points (16 equations):

  • Rsoil (1/Gs): the ionic path through the medium. Lower means wetter, or saltier.
  • Cm: both solder masks in series. The board fixes it, so a change means contact changed.
  • Cd: capacitance that never reaches the medium, such as board fringe or a hand around the board.

It runs Levenberg–Marquardt on the logarithms of the parameters from four starting guesses for Gs (10 µS to 10 mS) and keeps the best. The model includes the harmonic folding from step 4, so the high-frequency points do not have to be pure H(f).

The ± is the fit's own statistical spread. This simulation has only ADC noise; on board 1, model error widened it to ±11 % in the towel and ±17 % for the hand.

moisture runs the same fit and corrects to 25 °C with the tip NTC: Rsoil25 = Rsoil × (1 + 0.02·(T − 25)).

Firmware: specModel(), specFit(), cmdSpectrum(), lines 801–1109.

Step 1 of 7

How this is simulated. Waveforms and per-phase readings come from a time-domain simulation of the SENSE node (RK4, ideal square edges, 0.5 mV of ADC noise per reading). The sweep points in steps 6 and 7 use the firmware's own lock-in model (specModel) at each medium's parameters, with the same reading noise pushed through the same phase sums.

Why not the raw simulation there: specModel keeps harmonics only up to |h| = 63. Against ideal square edges that leaves a 0.2–0.5 % error, which the fit absorbs as a false ~390 kΩ in air. Board 1 reads open in air, so real edge rates evidently keep that from mattering. This is a finding from the simulation, not a hardware measurement.
